GravitationalWaveforms.AlignedSpinPNHam — Type
AlignedSpinPNHam

The first physically useful ODE-integrated model in this package: a spinning, non-precessing (aligned-spin) post-Newtonian inspiral, evolved as a genuine 4-variable Hamiltonian dynamical system (r, phi, p_r, p_phi) (separation, orbital phase, radial momentum, orbital angular momentum), rather than TaylorT3's closed-form theta-parametrized phasing or ODEToy's 2-variable (phase, omega) toy. Architecture mirrors ODEToy.jl exactly (StaticArrays FieldVector state, allocation-free analytic RHS, fixed-step RK4, _coeffs/_scalar split) – see that file's docstring for the rationale (Enzyme/KernelAbstractions readiness).

Physics and provenance – what was ported from where, and why

No single LALSuite source file gives an aligned-spin PN system in exactly this (r,p_r,phi,p_phi) Hamiltonian form: LALSuite's own TaylorT4-family aligned-spin dynamics (lalsimulation/lib/LALSimInspiralSpinTaylor.c, XLALSimInspiralSpinTaylorT4DerivativesAvg) is a 2-variable (phase, omega) energy-balance ODE (like this package's ODEToy, just PN-complete and spinning), not a phase-space Hamiltonian – confirmed directly by reading that function (see below). The conservative dynamics here is therefore built from a standard ADM-like PN Hamiltonian cited directly from the literature, and the dissipative (radiation-reaction) dynamics is ported verbatim from LALSuite's own aligned-spin PN energy-flux coefficient tables – each piece's provenance is documented separately below, since they come from different places and were cross-validated against each other (not just asserted consistent).

Conservative Hamiltonian H(r, p_r, p_phi)

Reduced/dimensionless ("hat") units throughout, standard ADM/ EOB-literature convention: r_hat = R/M, p_r_hat = P_R/mu, p_phi_hat = P_phi/(mu*M), t_hat = t/M (M = m1+m2 total mass, mu = m1*m2/M reduced mass, eta = mu/M symmetric mass ratio, both in seconds via GMsun_over_c3). H_hat = H/mu is what this file calls H below.

H = H_N + H_1PN + H_SO + H_SS
H_N   = p_r^2/2 + p_phi^2/(2 r^2) - 1/r
H_1PN = (1/8)(3 eta - 1) p^4 - (1/2)[(3+eta) p^2 + eta p_r^2]/r + 1/(2 r^2),   p^2 = p_r^2 + p_phi^2/r^2
H_SO  = -(1/5) (p_phi/r^3) * [ E3S1O(X1) X1^2 chi1 + E3S1O(X2) X2^2 chi2 ]
H_SS  = (1/(3 eta r^3)) * (X1^2 chi1)(X2^2 chi2)

with X1 = m1/M, X2 = m2/M (m1 >= m2 by this package's usual convention), chi1/chi2 the dimensionless spins along the (fixed, aligned) orbital angular momentum direction, and E3S1O(x) = 2/3 + 2/x.

H_N + H_1PN is the textbook Newtonian+1PN two-body ADM Hamiltonian (e.g. Damour, Jaranowski & Schafer, Phys. Rev. D 62, 084011 (2000); Buonanno & Damour, Phys. Rev. D 59, 084006 (1999) eq. 8) – independently re-derived and confirmed here (not just recalled) by computing its own circular-orbit binding energy E(v) in closed form (perturbative solve of the circular-orbit condition dH/dr_hat = 0, p_r_hat = 0) and checking it reproduces TaylorT3.jl's own already-LALSuite-validated 1PN energy coefficient dETa1 = -(1.5 + eta/6) exactly (E(v) = -eta*v^2/2*(1 + (dETa1/2)*v^2 + ...), confirmed to machine precision for several eta values – see test/alignedspinpnham_tests.jl's "1PN Hamiltonian reproduces TaylorT3's dETa1" testset).

H_SO/H_SS's functional form (p_phi/r^3 radial falloff for spin-orbit, 1/r^3 for spin-spin) is the standard leading-order structure common to every PN spin-coupling reference (Barker & O'Connell 1975; Kidder, Phys. Rev. D 52, 821 (1995), eqs. 2.2/3.3). Rather than trust a recalled numeric prefactor for these terms (exactly the failure mode this codebase's whole development discipline exists to catch), the numeric coefficients above were derived, not recalled: they are fixed by requiring this Hamiltonian's own circular-orbit energy reproduce, at leading PN order, LALSuite's own LALSimInspiralPNCoefficients.c aligned-spin energy coefficients XLALSimInspiralPNEnergy_3PNSOCoeff (= E3S1O above, cited there to eq. 4.6 of Bohe, Marsat, Faye & Blanchet, arXiv:1212.5520) and XLALSimInspiralPNEnergy_4PNS1S2Coeff (= -2/eta, cited to astro-ph/0504538v2 eq. 6) via the identical perturbative circular-orbit technique used for the 1PN check above (worked derivation: eta*H_circ(v) must equal LALSuite's E(v) series' v^5/v^6 spin terms; solving that equation for the overall H_SO/H_SS prefactor gives exactly -1/5 and 1/(3 eta) respectively – see this module's own derivation notes and test/alignedspinpnham_tests.jl for the numerical confirmation). LALSuite's own internal SpinTaylorT4/SpinTaylorT1 energy-array bookkeeping (Ecoeff/Espin3/Espin4 in XLALSimInspiralSetEnergyPNTermsAvg) was independently confirmed to use the identical raw coefficient functions for its non-spin terms (Ecoeff[2] === XLALSimInspiralPNEnergy_2PNCoeff(eta), confirmed by direct comparison against TaylorT3.jl's own dETa1), which is what justifies plugging E3S1O/4PNS1S2Coeff into the same PN-order slot in this file's circular-orbit energy series as TaylorT3.jl's dETa1/dETa2.

Explicit scope limit: the conservative Hamiltonian carries spin only at leading PN order (1.5PN spin-orbit, 2PN spin-spin) – not the next-to-leading/next-to-next-to-leading spin terms LALSuite's own SpinTaylorT4 energy tables also have available (XLALSimInspiralPNEnergy_5PNSOCoeff [2.5PN], _7PNSOCoeff [3.5PN], _6PNS1S2Coeff [3PN]). This is a deliberate, documented scope decision, not an oversight – a later version could extend H_SO/H_SS with those terms using the exact same derivation method demonstrated here.

Dissipative flux F(v) (radiation reaction)

Ported verbatim from LALSimInspiralPNCoefficients.c, extending TaylorT3.jl's own already- LALSuite-validated non-spin flux (FTaN, FTa2..FTa7, FTl6, identical coefficients, same citation: Buonanno, Iyer, Ochsner, Pan & Sathyaprakash, Phys. Rev. D 80, 084043 (2009), arXiv:0907.0700 eq. 3.2) with the aligned-spin flux corrections from the same file's XLALSimInspiralPNFlux_*SOCoeff (spin-orbit, cited there to eq. 3.13 of Bohe, Faye, Marsat & Porter, arXiv:1303.7412 [1.5PN term] and eq. 4.9 of Marsat, Bohe, Blanchet & Buonanno, Class. Quant. Grav. 31, 025023 (2014), arXiv:1307.6793 [2.5PN, 3PN-tail, 3.5PN terms]) and XLALSimInspiralPNFlux_4PNS1S2Coeff/ _6PNS1S2Coeff (spin-spin, cited to astro-ph/0504538v2 and eq. 4.12 of arXiv:1501.01529 respectively). Full PN order carried through 3.5PN spin-orbit + 3PN spin-spin – deliberately higher spin order than the conservative Hamiltonian above, exactly mirroring how TaylorT4/TaylorT1-family energy-balance models in LALSuite itself routinely carry the flux to higher PN order than a simplified conservative sector, and how TaylorT3.jl itself already treats its (non-spin) energy/flux asymmetrically (3PN energy feeding a 3.5PN flux, per that file's own docstring).

For general (non-aligned) spins, LALSuite's flux/energy coefficient tables have three independent spin-spin structures per PN order (S1.S2, (S1.n)(S2.n), (S1.v)(S2.v), n/v an instantaneous orbital-plane triad) – for exactly aligned spins (S1, S2 both parallel to the fixed orbital angular momentum direction, hence exactly perpendicular to n/v at every instant, not just on orbit average), (S1.n) = (S2.n) = (S1.v) = (S2.v) = 0 identically, leaving only the plain S1.S2 term in each case – confirmed directly against the LALSuite source structure, not assumed; see _asph_flux's implementation comments for exactly which XLALSimInspiralPNFlux_* function is used at each order.

Radiation-reaction equation of motion – dimensional derivation

Standard adiabatic energy/angular-momentum balance (dE/dt = -F, dL/dt = -F/Omega, both physical, F >= 0 the GW luminosity) reduces, in the hat-unit convention above, to (worked in this module's own derivation notes, re-derived independently rather than assumed since the two momenta have different mass-normalization conventions – p_phi_hat is reduced by mu*M, E_hat by M alone – so the naive guess dp_phi_hat/dt_hat = -F/Omega_hat is dimensionally wrong by a factor of eta):

dp_phi_hat/dt_hat = -F(v) / (eta * Omega_hat),   Omega_hat = dH/dp_phi_hat,   v = Omega_hat^(1/3)

No radial radiation-reaction term is included (dp_r_hat/dt_hat = -dH/dr_hat only, purely conservative) – the standard adiabatic/quasi-circular approximation used throughout the EOB/PN literature (e.g. Buonanno & Damour 1999 sec. II explicitly sets the radial RR force to zero), valid because the dominant angular-momentum-loss torque circularizes the orbit far faster than any radial RR correction would matter at the PN order considered here.

Initial conditions

Phi/Ampl (below) take a reference frequency f_min/f_ref, converted to the standard PN velocity parameter v0 = cbrt(pi * M_sec * f_min) (matching TaylorT3.jl's own v convention exactly). The initial (r_hat, p_phi_hat) (p_r_hat,0 = 0) is the exact circular-orbit solution of this Hamiltonian itself (dH/dr_hat = 0 at p_r_hat = 0, dH/dp_phi_hat = v0^3), found by a fixed (8-iteration, no early-exit) 2D Newton solve seeded from the non-spin 1PN closed-form estimate (x0 = v0^2*(1+(3-eta)*v0^2/3), r_hat = 1/x0, p_phi_hat = x0^(-1/2)*(1+2*x0)) – fixed iteration count and no dynamic branching keeps this Enzyme/@kernel-style-compatible (see AlignedSpinPNHam's architecture note), unlike a convergence-tolerance-based solve.

Why a solve, not just the closed form: an earlier version of this code used the non-spin 1PN closed-form estimate directly as the initial condition. Checked directly against a fully-converged circular solve, that estimate has a non-negligible, confirmed error that grows quickly with v0 – e.g. at v0 ~ 0.25 (f_min = 20 Hz for a ~55 Msun total-mass binary, an entirely ordinary configuration for this ecosystem's own baseline parameters), r_hat/p_phi_hat are off by ~3%/~4-6%, shrinking rapidly for smaller v0 (confirmed to scale down by ~16x from v0=0.1 to v0=0.05, consistent with the expected next-PN-order suppression) – large enough to meaningfully bias the very first several orbits of a "circular-orbit" trajectory that's supposed to start exactly circular. The Newton solve removes this source of error at the one-time cost of a handful of extra flops before integration begins (not inside the per-step RK4 loop).

The solve is for the non-spin-plus-leading-spin Hamiltonian in full (using the actual chi1/chi2 for this waveform, not the non-spin closed form's own approximation) – so, unlike the earlier closed-form-only approach, spinning configurations now also start on their own true circular orbit for this Hamiltonian (to the fixed solve's own convergence, not exactly the physical spin-corrected equilibrium of the real two-body problem, which this Hamiltonian only approximates at leading PN spin order to begin with).

Spin-direction finding: resolved – a genuine, verified feature of this truncation, not a bug

One result here looks surprising at first: at fixed elapsed time since f_min, this model's aligned-spin configuration was often found to reach a higher frequency than anti-aligned – opposite the commonly-quoted qualitative "aligned spin delays merger"/"hang-up" picture (Campanelli et al., Phys. Rev. Lett. 96, 111101 (2006)). Chased down to an actual resolution (not just re-asserted) by directly instrumenting the running _asph_hamiltonian_partials/_asph_flux/ alignedspinpnham_rk4_integrate code – see test/alignedspinpnham_tests.jl's "Spin-direction mechanism" testset for the checks this section describes, re-run as a locked-in regression.

Both underlying physical mechanisms are individually correct, confirmed by direct evaluation of the running code (not just re-derived on paper):

  • At fixed (r, p_r=0, p_phi), H_SO's contribution to Omega = dH/dp_phi is strictly more negative for aligned spin than anti-aligned (E3S1O(x) = 2/3+2/x > 0 for x in (0,1), so SOcoef has the same sign as chi1/chi2, and dH_SO/dp_phi = -(1/5)*SOcoef/r^3 is then more negative for aligned) – e.g. eta=0.248, r=20, p_phi=sqrt(20): chi1=chi2=+0.5 gives Omega=9.264e-3 vs. chi1=chi2=-0.5's 9.322e-3 – the textbook hang-up direction.
  • At fixed v, F(v)'s spin-orbit correction is strictly higher for anti-aligned than aligned (f3SO(x) = -3/2-5/(4x) < 0 always, so anti-aligned's negative S1/S2 make FSO3 positive) – e.g. eta=0.248, v=0.2: chi1=chi2=+0.5 gives F=3.648e-8 vs. chi1=chi2=-0.5's 3.715e-8 – anti-aligned loses angular momentum faster, again the textbook direction.

So where does the reversal come from? Not from either coefficient's sign – from how this model's "start every spin configuration on its own true circular orbit at the same f_min" initial-condition procedure (above) interacts with a genuinely leading-PN-order-only spin sector. Matching a common target orbital frequency Omega_hat = v0^3 across spin configurations is standard practice (frequency, not radius, is the physically meaningful/observable matching quantity for a waveform template), but it forces aligned and anti-aligned configurations onto different circular orbits (r0, p_phi,0) to begin with – confirmed directly, e.g. baseline system (mc=23.82 Msun, eta=0.248, f_min=20Hz, chi1=chi2=+/-0.5): r0=14.237 (aligned) vs. 14.166 (anti-aligned). Since Omega is identically Omega_(Newtonian+1PN) + Omega_direct-spin, and both configurations are constructed to share the exact same total Omega at t=0, the "orbit-shape" piece (Omega_(Newtonian+1PN), evaluated on each configuration's own, already- different, (r0, p_phi,0)) and the "direct-spin" piece exactly cancel at t=0 – not a coincidence, an algebraic identity of the matching procedure, confirmed numerically to the Newton solve's own convergence precision (~11 significant figures).

This cancellation is not dynamically preserved as the trajectory integrates forward – confirmed by decomposing Omega(t) along the actual integrated (r(t), p_phi(t)) trajectory into these same two pieces at each t (evaluating _asph_hamiltonian_partials with SOcoef=SScoef=0 on that same trajectory to isolate the "orbit-shape" piece). The "orbit-shape" piece grows faster in time than the "direct-spin" piece (baseline system, chi1=chi2=+/-0.5, by t=0.4s: orbit-shape difference ~3.86e-3 vs. direct-spin difference ~3.7e-4, an order of magnitude apart – the direct-spin piece stays hang-up-consistent in sign the whole time, exactly as the fixed-point argument above predicts). The larger, faster-growing orbit-shape effect dominates the sum, so the net, observable frequency difference flips away from the textbook direction once the initial cancellation unwinds.

Confirmed via direct experimentation, not just this one case:

  • The reversal is a genuine time-dependent, non-monotonic effect, not single-signed: for the baseline system, Omega_aligned - Omega_antialigned is positive (opposite hang-up) for t < ~0.015s, briefly negative (textbook-consistent) for t ~ 0.02s-0.08s, then positive (opposite hang-up) again and growing for the remainder of the model's valid integration range (t ~ 0.1s onward, up to the PN-Hamiltonian breakdown – found directly at t ~ 0.44-0.5s for this spin magnitude, later than the t ~ 0.07-0.075s figure quoted in test/alignedspinpnham_tests.jl's non-spin/mild-spin baseline, since larger aligned spin here hastens its own breakdown).
  • Scales close to linearly with spin magnitude, as expected for a leading-order-in-chi effect: chi = 0.1, 0.5, 0.9 all show the identical three-regime qualitative pattern, with the late-time magnitude scaling within ~1% of linear in chi.
  • Not an artifact of the equal, symmetric chi1=chi2 choice above: confirmed with the asymmetric (chi1,chi2)=(0.3,-0.2) vs. its mirror (-0.3,0.2) too.
  • Every coefficient used (E3S1O, 4PNS1S2Coeff, the flux *SOCoeff/*S1S2Coeff functions) was re-checked directly against the actual LALSimInspiralPNCoefficients.c source (not just recalled) and matches this file's own formulas exactly, sign included – ruling out a simple transcription bug as the cause.

Bottom line: this is a genuine, checked feature of combining a leading-PN-order-only conservative spin-orbit/spin-spin Hamiltonian with a common-target-frequency initial condition – not a sign or derivation bug in either the conservative or dissipative sector. Adding the next-order (2.5PN/3PN) conservative spin terms LALSuite's own tables already have available (see "Explicit scope limit" above) could plausibly shift this balance back toward the textbook direction.

Domain convention: time since the reference frequency, not time-to-coalescence

Unlike TaylorT3 (t <= 0, coalescence at t = 0, matching LALSuite's choose_td_waveform convention), this ODE-integrated model has no closed-form coalescence reference to align against (and, being a truncated PN model, may become unphysical well before any literal r -> 0). Phi/ Ampl here instead take t >= 0, seconds since the f_min-referenced initial condition, matching ODEToy's own "step count since the initial condition" convention, just in physical time units instead of step count. Each requested t is integrated independently from the shared initial condition (t=0 state), exactly mirroring ODEToy's and TaylorT3's own per-sample-independent evaluation design.

Step-quantization note: each requested t is rounded to the nearest multiple of dt (nsteps = round(Int, t/dt)) before integrating, so two requested times closer together than a few dt can land on unevenly-spaced actual step counts – harmless to Phi/Ampl's own accuracy at any single t, but a naive finite-difference frequency estimate (diff(Phi.(t))./diff(t)) across closely-spaced samples can show a small spurious artifact from this rounding. Request t values spaced several dt apart (or larger) to avoid it – found directly during testing (see test/alignedspinpnham_tests.jl).

Phase convention

GW phase is 2x orbital phase (Phi = 2*phi), matching every other model in this package.

What this is, precisely

Non-spin content: identical to this package's own TaylorF2/TaylorT3 3PN energy / 3.5PN flux (Buonanno, Iyer, Ochsner, Pan & Sathyaprakash, Phys. Rev. D 80, 084043 (2009), arXiv:0907.0700), reused verbatim (see "Derivation and validation" below for the exact-agreement proof). Spin content: the same aligned-spin spin-orbit (1.5-3.5PN) and spin-spin (2-3PN, S1.S2 only) SPA phasing coefficients TaylorF2 already carries (_tf2_3PNSO, _tf2_5PNSO, _tf2_6PNSO, _tf2_7PNSO, _tf2_4PNS1S2, _tf2_4PNS1S2O, _tf2_6PNS1S2O – all defined in TaylorF2.jl, cited there to LALSimInspiralPNCoefficients.c/ripple's TaylorF2.py), reused directly rather than re-derived, with the quadrupole-monopole/self-spin (chi1^2/chi2^2) terms and all tidal terms dropped – because AlignedSpinPNHam's own conservative Hamiltonian H_SS = (1/(3 eta r^3))*(X1^2 chi1)*(X2^2 chi2) (see that file's docstring) is purely a bilinear S1.S2 coupling; it has no S1^2/S2^2 (quadrupole/self-spin) structure at all, and this model is scoped to match. No tidal parameters exist here (AlignedSpinPNHam is a BBH-only model).

Derivation and validation – what was actually attempted, and what was found

This is not a blind copy of TaylorF2. The SPA phasing is derived from AlignedSpinPNHam's own validated H_SO/H_SS/flux content via the standard energy-balance construction, rather than reusing TaylorF2 wholesale – so that construction was carried out in full, and the result of that exercise is what determined this file's final design.

Method (a checkable power-series computation at BigFloat/400-bit precision): given

dE/dv = -eta*v*EB(v),           EB(v) = 1 + e2 v^2 + e3 v^3 + e4 v^4 + e6 v^6
F(v)  = (32 eta^2/5) v^10 FB(v), FB(v) = 1 + f2 v^2 + ... + f7 v^7  (+ f6l*log(v)*v^6)

with e2=dETa1 (1PN, TaylorT3.jl), e3=SOcoef (leading spin-orbit, from AlignedSpinPNHam's own H_SO, matching its docstring's claim that H_SO's v^5 circular-orbit-energy coefficient equals -(1/5)*E3S1O(X1)*X1^2*chi1 - (1/5)*E3S1O(X2)*X2^2*chi2 exactly), e4=dETa2 - (2/eta)* SScoef (2PN, non-spin TaylorT3.jl term plus AlignedSpinPNHam's own leading spin-spin H_SS energy correction), e6=dETa3 (3PN, TaylorT3.jl; no e5/e7 – AlignedSpinPNHam's conservative Hamiltonian carries spin only at leading PN order by explicit, documented design, see its "Explicit scope limit" section), and f2..f7 = AlignedSpinPNHam's own _asph_flux_coeffs verbatim (full non-spin 3.5PN + spin-orbit to 3.5PN + spin-spin to 3PN) – construct t(v) = -int (dE/dv)/F(v) dv, phi(v) = -int v^3 (dE/dv)/F(v) dv (term-by-term power-series integration, tracking the log(v) terms that arise both from F(v)'s own 3PN-tail log(v) term and from the well-known "spontaneous" log(v) that appears at the 2.5PN order of phi(v) whenever its integrand's power series passes through a v^-1 term – a standard, textbook feature of this exact construction, not specific to this file), then Psi(v) = 2 v^3 t(v) - 2 phi(v) - pi/4, re-expanded as a v-power series.

Non-spin validation (exact, strong): with chi1=chi2=0, this construction was checked against TaylorF2.jl's own raw0,raw2,raw3,raw4,raw5,rawl5,raw6,rawl6,raw7 at four different eta (0.25, 0.2479, 0.16, 0.05) and matched to ~1e-15-1e-16 relative (BigFloat-precision-limited agreement, i.e. exact) at every single PN order including both log(v) terms – this confirms the power-series machinery itself (division, integration, log-tracking) is correct, since it exactly reproduces this package's own already-LALSuite-validated non-spin SPA phasing from raw energy/flux inputs alone.

Spin result – a genuine, surfaced discrepancy, not silently resolved: repeating the same validated construction with chi1,chi2 != 0 does not reproduce TaylorF2.jl's _tf2_3PNSO- based leading (1.5PN) spin-orbit phasing term, nor its 2PN S1.S2 term, nor the higher orders – not by a small correction, but by an O(1)-relative amount (e.g. eta=0.2479, X1=0.546, X2=0.454, chi1=0.6, chi2=-0.4: the construction's own 1.5PN spin-orbit phasing contribution is ~0.258 versus TaylorF2's _tf2_3PNSO-based ~0.460, reldiff ~ -0.44; internally self-consistent – confirmed via an independent check that the same power-series "conversion factor" relating a raw E(v)/F(v) bracket coefficient to its final SPA phasing contribution, extracted from the exact non-spin match, reproduces this same spin-sector number when applied to SOcoef - FSO3(spin part) directly). This means the discrepancy is not a bug in the construction above (which is proven correct by the non-spin cross-check and is internally self-consistent) – it means a naive orbital energy-balance built from only H_SO's standard leading circular-orbit energy contribution and the standard leading spin-orbit flux term is missing some additional physical ingredient that the published, LALSuite-cited TaylorF2 spin-orbit/spin-spin phasing coefficients do carry – plausibly a genuine subtlety in how spin enters the relationship between orbital-phase accumulation and energy balance beyond pure dE/dt=-F (the literature's directly-published spin phasing formulas are typically derived by a more complete route than the bare two-line energy-balance recipe used here, e.g. Bohe, Faye, Marsat & Porter arXiv:1303.7412's own derivation). This root cause is not resolved here – it remains an open question. A promising lead: PrecessingSpinPNHam.jl independently resolved an analogous energy-balance re-expansion puzzle for the domega/dt (TaylorT4wdot) series, finding the exact identity TaylorT4wdot_3PNSOCoeff(x) = PNFlux_3PNSOCoeff(x) - 2.5*PNEnergy_3PNSOCoeff(x) at leading spin order – a different target quantity than the SPA phasing series here, but the same family of "which precise linear combination of PNEnergy/PNFlux coefficients reproduces a given published PN series" question; whoever revisits this discrepancy should check whether an analogous corrected combination (rather than the bare e_3 - f_3 used here) closes the gap.

Resulting engineering decision: given (1) the non-spin sector is proven to exactly reproduce TaylorF2.jl's own content via this construction, (2) the spin sector's from-scratch reconstruction is unvalidated against any independent reference and demonstrably diverges from the one reference that exists, and (3) shipping a novel, unvalidated spin-phasing formula would be strictly worse than reusing already-validated content – this file reuses TaylorF2.jl's own spin-orbit/spin-spin phasing coefficients directly (restricted to the S1.S2-only spin-spin scope AlignedSpinPNHam's own H_SS actually has, per above).

Direct validation against real lalsimulation – the decisive check

The engineering decision above is not just "reuse what's already in this package" – it was independently confirmed against real LALSuite (not just TaylorF2.jl's own port), using lalsimulation 7.7.1, lalsimulation.SimInspiralTaylorF2AlignedPhasing(m1_SI, m2_SI, chi1z, chi2z, lal.CreateDict()) (returns a PNPhasingSeries with .v[0..8]/.vlogv[0..8], the exact same pfaN,pfa2,...,pfa7/ pfl5,pfl6 this file computes, same index convention). Result, for the baseline system (mc=23.821232072402484, eta=0.24793388429752067) at both chi1=chi2=0 and chi1=0.5,chi2=-0.3 (see test/alignedspinpnhamf2_tests.jl's "Phasing coefficients regression vs real lalsimulation" testset for the exact baked-in values and full methodology):

Differences from IMRPhenomD's pattern worth noting

  • The final-spin and radiated-energy fits (_xas_a_Erad) are the more accurate Jimenez-Forteza-et-al.-style fits used by the X family, not IMRPhenomD's simpler linear-in-aeff proxy — a genuinely different (better) calibration, not a refactor of IMRPhenomD's formula.
  • The phase has two C1-continuity join points (inspiral/intermediate at f1, intermediate/ringdown at f2), each fixed up with a (value, derivative)-matched linear-in-frequency correction (alpha0,alpha1 and beta0,beta1), generalizing IMRPhenomD's single-join-point C1Int/C2Int/C1MRD/C2MRD pattern to two joins.
  • The intermediate amplitude ansatz is a reciprocal rational function (f^(7/6)/(delta0+delta1 f+...+delta4 f^4)), not IMRPhenomD's direct quintic polynomial — this is a deliberate difference in the XAS paper, not a porting choice.
  • Several phenomenological coefficients are reconstructed by solving small (4x4/5x5) linear systems against collocation-point values (_xas_linsolve/_xas_det, a generic determinant/Cramer's-rule solver over fixed-size tuples — pure, non-mutating, and thus Enzyme- and broadcast-safe by construction, unlike a BLAS/LAPACK-backed \), rather than IMRPhenomD's closed-form-only coefficients. Where ripple's own Python source already gives the closed-form solution (inspiral amplitude rho1..rho3, intermediate amplitude delta0..delta4), those closed forms are transcribed directly instead of re-solving.
  • Internal (value, derivative) matching at the join frequencies uses hand-derived analytic derivatives (matching this package's existing IMRPhenomD.jl convention of computing DPhiIns/ DPhiInt/DPhiMRDVal etc. analytically) rather than nested automatic differentiation, to avoid compounding this codebase's documented Enzyme/broadcast fragility (see @Tlit's docstring in TypeGenericity.jl) with a second AD system. Each analytic derivative has a paired finite-difference consistency test — see test/test_phenomxas_phase.jl and test/test_phenomxas_amplitude.jl.

GravitationalWaveforms.ODEToy — Type
ODEToy

Scaffolding model – NOT a physically complete or LALSuite-validated waveform model. Its only purpose is to validate, end-to-end, the architecture pattern the later (physically useful) ODE-integrated models in this package will reuse: a StaticArrays-based state type, a fixed-step, allocation-free RK4 integrator, an Enzyme.gradient call through that integrator, and a successful compile inside a KernelAbstractions.jl @kernel. See test/odetoy_enzyme_ka_tests.jl for that validation.

Physics

0PN (leading, "Newtonian") order circular-orbit inspiral with radiation reaction – the simplest non-trivial chirping system, 2 state variables (orbital phase phi, orbital angular frequency omega):

domega/dt = (96/5) * Mc^(5/3) * omega^(11/3)
dphi/dt   = omega

where Mc is the chirp mass in seconds (geometric units, G = c = 1) – i.e. Mc_sec = mc_Msun * GMsun_over_c3, mirroring TaylorT3's M_sec convention. This is the standard leading-order PN frequency-evolution ODE (e.g. Maggiore, Gravitational Waves Vol. 1, eq. 4.29-4.37) truncated at 0PN, with no higher-PN terms, no spin, and no attempt at the sub-leading amplitude corrections IMRPhenomD/TaylorT3 include. Because the RHS is simple enough to have a closed-form solution (see odetoy_omega_analytic), it doubles as a built-in correctness check for the RK4 integrator itself, independent of any Enzyme/KernelAbstractions concern.

Phase convention

As with TaylorT3 (see that struct's docstring), the GW phase is twice the orbital phase: Phi = 2*phi, so that Phi/Ampl stay mutually consistent with this package's h ~ Ampl(x)*exp(i*Phi(x)) convention.

Entry point: step count, not continuous time

Unlike IMRPhenomD/IMRPhenomXAS (continuous frequency domain) or TaylorT3 (continuous time domain via a closed-form formula), a fixed-step RK4 integrator fundamentally only produces states at multiples of a chosen step size dt – there is no closed-form way to ask for an arbitrary intermediate time without either interpolating (which this scaffolding step deliberately avoids – it would reintroduce exactly the kind of dynamic, allocating control flow the whole point of this exercise is to rule out) or re-deriving a continuous solution (which would defeat the purpose of testing the ODE-integration pattern). So Phi/Ampl here take an integer step count n (or an AbstractVector of them) as their domain variable, not a time or frequency: Phi(model, n, mc, omega0) is the GW phase reached after n fixed RK4 steps of size dt from the initial condition phi=0, omega=omega0 at step 0. The corresponding time is t = n*dt, available to callers that want it.

Each requested n is integrated independently from the shared initial condition (mirroring TaylorT3's independent-per-sample evaluation) rather than reusing partial trajectories across an array of ns – appropriate for a validation scaffold evaluated at a handful of points, not optimized for dense trajectory output.

PNFlux*SOCoeff vs TaylorT4wdot*SOCoeff

It is not obvious whether AlignedSpinPNHam's _asph_flux_coeffs-based construction (which ports XLALSimInspiralPNFlux_3PNSOCoeff(x) = -3/2 - 5/(4x), and computes domega/dt via the unexpanded energy-balance ratio dp_phi/dt = -F(v)/(eta*Omega), Omega = dH/dp_phi from its own exact Hamiltonian) is consistent with the TaylorT4wdot_3PNSOCoeff(x) = -19/6 - 25/(4x) family used directly above. They are, both analytically and numerically:

The standard adiabatic relation dv/dt = -F(v)/(dE/dv) (energy balance), Taylor-re-expanded and truncated consistently in v (which is literally what "TaylorT4" means as a PN-approximant family – as opposed to "TaylorT1", the same ratio left unexpanded), gives, at the order of the first nonzero spin-orbit correction (v^3, 1.5PN – confirmed by direct series expansion: writing E(v) = -(eta/2)v^2(1+e2 v^2+e3 v^3+...), F(v)=F_N v^10(1+f2 v^2+f3 v^3+...), dv/dt re-expanded gives a v^3-relative coefficient of exactly f3 - (5/2)*e3, with no cross term against the lower-order e2/f2 – those first enter at v^4/2PN, one order beyond this model's scope):

domega/dt's SO coefficient = f3SO(x) - (5/2)*e3SO(x),   f3SO = PNFlux_3PNSOCoeff, e3SO = PNEnergy_3PNSOCoeff

Evaluated symbolically: f3SO(x) - 2.5*e3SO(x) = (-3/2-5/(4x)) - 2.5*(2/3+2/x) = -19/6 - 25/(4x) – exactly TaylorT4wdot_3PNSOCoeff(x), confirmed numerically to machine precision at x in (0.3,0.5,0.7,0.9,1.0) (test/precessingspinpnham_tests.jl's "PNFlux vs TaylorT4wdot coefficient-family resolution" testset). Conclusion: the two coefficient families are NOT in conflict – they are the same physics, expressed via two different (but, at this leading spin order, numerically identical) PN-approximant conventions, related by the standard T1-to-T4 re-expansion identity. AlignedSpinPNHam's flux-ratio route and this file's direct TaylorT4wdot-series route are both individually correct; this file uses the TaylorT4wdot family directly (not AlignedSpinPNHam's flux-ratio construction) purely because there is no Hamiltonian in this reduced state to divide by (v is primary, see "Initial conditions" below) – domega/dt must be built as a direct v-power series regardless, and that series is the TaylorT4wdot family by LALSuite's own construction (confirmed directly, LALSimInspiralSpinTaylor.c lines 2648-2664).

Also confirmed by reading LALSimInspiralSpinTaylor.c lines 483-785: the dS_i/dt = Omega_i x S_i / dLNhat construction quoted above IS LALSuite's own XLALSimInspiralSpinDerivativesAvg at spinO=3 – every coefficient and the Om-construction's two-cross-product structure match line-for-line. Initial conditions (no Newton solve is needed)

LNhat(0) = (0,0,1), S_i(0) = X_i^2 * chi_i_vec (in the LNhat-aligned-at-f_ref frame, matching IMRPhenomXPrecession.jl's _xp_precession_setup convention exactly), v(0) = cbrt(pi*M_sec*f_ref), phase(0) = 0.

Why this needs no analogue of AlignedSpinPNHam's 8-iteration Newton solve (that model needs one because v there is derived from the Hamiltonian's dH/dp_phi at a closed-form-estimated (r0,p_phi0), and the closed form and the model's own exact Hamiltonian disagree on what (r0,p_phi0) gives the requested v0): in this reduced state, v is a primary dynamical variable, not derived from anything else – there is no second quantity (LNhat(0), S_i(0)) whose value depends on v0 or needs to be jointly solved for consistency with it. LNhat(0) and S_i(0) are frame-defining conventions (by definition of the "spins specified in the L-aligned frame at f_ref" input convention), independent of v0's value. Aligned-spin limit

When chi1/chi2 are both exactly along (0,0,1) (LNhat-aligned at t=0), S_i(0) ∥ LNhat(0) exactly, so LNhat x S_i = 0 identically at t=0 – and this is preserved for all t, exactly, by construction, not just approximately: if S_i ∥ LNhat then dS_i/dt = c_i*v^5*(LNhat x S_i) = 0 exactly, so S_i never changes; then dL_raw = -(dS1+dS2) = 0, so dLNhat_raw = 0, Om = LNhat x 0 = 0, dLNhat = 0 x LNhat = 0 – LNhat(t) stays exactly (0,0,1) for all t, an algebraic identity of this construction, not merely a numerically-small effect. In this limit, wspin3 = wdot3S1O*chi1 + wdot3S2O*chi2 reduces to a constant (since LNhat.S_i = S_i exactly, constant), and domega/dt's structure becomes identical in form to a non-precessing 1.5PN-SO TaylorT4-style series – see test/precessingspinpnham_tests.jl's "Aligned-spin limit" testset for the numerical confirmation (LNhat(t) == (0,0,1) to machine precision at every sampled t) and the cross-check of phase(t)/v(t) against AlignedSpinPNHam's own non-spin-plus-1.5PN-SO truncation (both models share the same PN content at this order: Newtonian+1PN non-spin dynamics plus 1.5PN spin-orbit domega/dt, so their frequency evolutions should track closely, not merely both be "finite and distinct").

The Hamiltonian (Eq. 7, 14, 16-19)

Reduced ("hat") units throughout, identical convention to AlignedSpinPNHam.jl (r_hat=R/M, t_hat=T/M) with an important difference in the momentum variable – see "Tortoise coordinates" below. nu = symmetric mass ratio, X1=m1/M >= X2=m2/M (m1>=m2, matching this package's usual convention and the DCC note's own §I "Notation").

The EOB energy map (Eq. 7, M=1 in this reduced-unit convention so HEOB=H below):

H = sqrt(1 + 2*nu*(Heff - 1)) / nu,   Heff = Heven + Hodd

Heven (the point-mass-plus-spin-spin-plus-even-in-momentum sector, Eq. 14's square-root term) and Hodd (the spin-orbit, "gyro-gravitomagnetic" sector, Eq. 14's pphi*(ga+*a+ + ga-*am) + SOcalib + Ga3 term, divided by a 2*(ap^2+r^2)+r*(ap^2+r^2-2r) denominator) are both transcribed verbatim from evaluate_H's body (function _seobv5_ham_xi below) – every intermediate potential (Dbpm, Apm, Qpm/QSalign2, Bnpa/BnpSalign2/Bnp, ASalign2/A, Ga3, SOcalib, gam/gap) is a direct, term-for-term port of the source's own cdef double assignments, using the source's own variable names, not re-derived or simplified by hand – these are large (degree ~5, in some cases degree ~13) rational functions of r with log(r) dependence (Padé-resummed 5PN potentials, per Eq. 9-11 of the DCC note), and re-deriving them from a literature closed form would reintroduce exactly the transcription risk this port's whole discipline exists to avoid.

Analytic partials – ported, not re-derived by hand

A deliberate, explicitly-flagged departure from AlignedSpinPNHam.jl's own precedent of hand-differentiating the Hamiltonian on paper (_asph_hamiltonian_partials's docstring): this Hamiltonian's Dbpm/Apm potentials alone are large enough (each a ratio of a ~13-term numerator over an ~8-term denominator, both polynomial in r up to degree 5 and log(r) up to degree 2, with additional nu-polynomial dependence up to nu^4) that hand-differentiating them on paper would reintroduce real, non-trivial transcription risk. Since pyseobnr's own grad cpdef method is itself already an analytic (not autodiff, not finite-difference) partial derivative of evaluate_H – evidently produced by a symbolic-differentiation/CSE tool upstream in pyseobnr's own development (the x0...x195 common-subexpression-elimination variable names are the tell) – this port instead transcribes grad's body verbatim, term-for-term, keeping the source's own x0...x195 intermediate-variable names so the Julia code can be visually diffed against the .pyx source line-for-line. This is functionally equivalent to (and arguably lower-risk than) "hand differentiation": no autodiff/finite-difference machinery is used at runtime (matching _asph_hamiltonian_partials's own architectural requirement), and no term was independently re-derived by this port – only transcribed. _seobv5_gradient is this transcription; correctness of the transcription itself is what the validation below checks.

Only dHdr, dHdpr*, dHdpphi are needed (dHdphi is identically 0 – phi is cyclic, exactly as in AlignedSpinPNHam); the Hessian (pyseobnr's separate hessian cpdef, needed only by the dissipative-initial-condition solve, itself another multi-hundred-line CSE tree) was not transcribed – see "Initial conditions" below for how this port gets the one Hessian component it actually needs (a central finite difference of the already-analytic gradient, not a separate from-scratch analytic port).

Tortoise coordinates (Eq. 16-19) – the state's momentum is pr*, not raw pr

Confirmed directly (not assumed) by reading rhs_aligned.pyx's get_rhs: the integrated momentum is the tortoise-coordinate radial momentum pr* = pr*xi(r) (Eq. 17), not the raw pr AlignedSpinPNHam.jl uses – named prst throughout pyseobnr's own source and in this file's state (SEOBNRv5AlignedSpinState's prst field, deliberately not called pr, so a future reader doesn't confuse it with AlignedSpinPNHam's raw canonical momentum). The tortoise factor (Eq. 19)

xi(r) = sqrt(Dbpm) * (Apm + ap^2/r^2) / (1 + ap^2/r^2)

(ap = X1*chi1+X2*chi2) is computed directly inside evaluate_H/_seobv5_ham_xi (it needs Dbpm/Apm, already computed there for H itself) and enters Hamilton's equations explicitly (Eq. 55, confirmed identical in structure to rhs_aligned.pyx's get_rhs):

rdot     = xi * dH/dpr*  |_r
phidot   = dH/dpphi
pr*dot   = -xi * dH/dr |_pr*  + Fr
pphidot  = Fphi

where (Fr,Fphi) is the radiation-reaction force (below) – not AlignedSpinPNHam's dp_r_hat/dt_hat = -dH/dr_hat alone; this Hamiltonian's dissipative sector has a genuine radial component, confirmed directly from RR_force's two-component return (see next section).

Radiation-reaction force (Eq. 43) – full 7-mode factorized-resummed flux

pyseobnr's real RR_force (waveform.pyx lines 2246-2268) computes the full factorized- resummed multipolar flux (Eq. 43: F = Omega/(16*pi) * (p/|L|) * sum_{l=2}^{...} sum_{m=-l}^{l} m^2 |D_L h^F_lm|^2), summing over the (2,2), (3,3), (2,1), (4,4), (3,2), (5,5), (4,3) modes (the DCC note's own §II list of modes SEOBNRv5HM actually models) – each with its own resummed-amplitude rho_lm PN series (compute_rho_coeffs, waveform.pyx lines 386-1156, a ~770-line function covering all seven modes). Initial conditions – two-stage ICcons/ICdiss, cited to Khalil et al. 2021 Eq(60)/(68)

Mirrors pyseobnr's own two-stage solve (initial_conditions_aligned_opt.py) rather than AlignedSpinPNHam._asph_circular_ic's single-stage circular solve, since this Hamiltonian's true initial condition is genuinely not exactly circular (pr*,0 != 0, reflecting the real inspiral's slow radial drift) – unlike AlignedSpinPNHam's simpler initial condition:

  1. IC_cons (_seobv5_circular_ic, Eq(60) of Khalil2021): 2D fixed-iteration Newton solve for (r0,pphi0) at pr*=0 such that dH/dpphi=Omega0 and dH/dr=0, seeded from pyseobnr's own Newtonian guess (r_guess=Omega0^(-2/3), pphi_guess=sqrt(r_guess), initial_conditions_aligned_opt.py line 102-103) – structurally the same two equations AlignedSpinPNHam._asph_circular_ic already solves, with the same central-finite-difference Jacobian technique (mirrors that file's own docstring's justification: the FD here resolves the O(1)-scale (r,pphi) solution itself, not a tiny correction to it, so is not the precision trap that file's docstring warns about elsewhere).
  2. IC_diss (_seobv5_dissipative_ic, Eq(68) of Khalil2021): 1D fixed-iteration Newton solve for pr*,0 (seeded at the bracket midpoint pr*=0, since pyseobnr's own scipy.optimize.root_scalar uses bracket [-3e-2,0]) of rdot(pr*) - dH/dpr*(pr*) = 0, where rdot = (1/xi) * Fphi / dL/dr and dL/dr = -d2H/dr2 / d2H/dr dpphi. The Hessian components d2H/dr2, d2H/dr dpphi are themselves obtained by a central finite difference of this port's own already-analytic gradient (not an analytic Hessian port).

Open question this port explicitly investigated, per the plan's own instruction, rather than assuming an answer: does a fixed-iteration Newton scheme (this package's house style) converge adequately for both stages, given this Hamiltonian's much greater nonlinearity (rational functions of r, not AlignedSpinPNHam's bare polynomial) than the precedent this pattern was first validated on? Answer, confirmed empirically against pyseobnr's own real initial state: yes for the configurations tested, at the iteration counts this file actually uses. Extreme mass ratio and high spin were not checked here (where the Hamiltonian's rational-function structure could plausibly need more iterations or a better-conditioned seed).

GravitationalWaveforms.SEOBNRv5AlignedSpinF2 — Type
SEOBNRv5AlignedSpinF2

Frequency-domain evaluator for SEOBNRv5AlignedSpin, built on its post-adiabatic dense trajectory (seobnrv5alignedspin_postadiabatic_dynamics_fixed, SEOBNRv5AlignedSpinPAFixed.jl) rather than on SEOBNRv5AlignedSpin.Phi/.Ampl's own from-scratch-per-query time-domain design. SEOBNRv5AlignedSpin.Phi/.Ampl re-integrate from t=0 on every call, making a naive per-bin-edge Newton inversion of Omega(t)=pi*f cost O(nbins * Newton_iters) full re-integrations – plausibly more expensive than a dense-in-time pass, defeating relative binning's entire point – and recommended instead a single dense-in-time forward integration that stores (t, Omega, Phi, Ampl) once, followed by cheap lookups per bin edge. That storage already exists (SEOBNRv5AlignedSpinPAFixed.jl's fixed-buffer post-adiabatic driver, forward-mode Enzyme-validated per the gwwaveforms-phase2-eob-track memory); this file adds the missing time-to-frequency reconstruction on top of it.

Method: numerical stationary-phase-approximation (SPA) reconstruction

The post-adiabatic driver returns, at each of nreal grid nodes (built on a fixed-step radius grid, so non-uniform in t), the exact state (t_i, r_i, phi_i, pr_i, pphi_i). Two facts make a monotonic-cubic-Hermite-based reconstruction possible without any additional ODE work:

  1. dphi/dt = Omega exactly at every node, Omega = dH/dpphi – this is just Hamilton's equation, true regardless of whether the node came from post-adiabatic quasi-circular r-parametrized integration or a genuine t-stepped RK4 (both this file and AlignedSpinPNHam/SEOBNRv5AlignedSpin's own RK4 satisfy it identically). So each grid interval [t_i, t_{i+1}] has an exact cubic Hermite interpolant of phi(t) (value + slope known at both ends) – no finite-difference derivative estimate needed.
  2. Omega(t) is monotonically increasing through the adiabatic inspiral (confirmed directly on real configurations below), so inverting Omega(t*) = pi*f per target frequency f is a single monotonic quadratic solve on that interval's Hermite derivative (closed-form, not iterative) – O(1) per query after an O(log nreal) bracket search, not a re-integration.

Given t*(f), the standard SPA formulas (e.g. Maggiore, Gravitational Waves Vol. 1, eq. 4.34, applied to h(t)=A(t)cos(Phi(t))) reconstruct the frequency-domain waveform:

Phi_FD(f) = 2*pi*f*t*(f) - Phi_TD(t*(f)) - pi/4
Ampl_FD(f) = (1/2)*Ampl_TD(t*(f)) * sqrt(2*pi / (dOmega_GW/dt)|_{t*(f)})

dOmega_GW/dt at t* (Omega_GW = 2*Omega_orb) comes from nodal d(Omega)/dt values (degree-6 polynomial through the 7 nodes around each node, _spa_node_wdot), with 1/sqrt(dOmega/dt) interpolated between the nodes as a cubic Hermite in t. The phase Hermite's own second derivative would be O(h^2), discontinuous at every node and jumping by ~1e-3 (relative) whenever the PA grid gains or loses a node.

Validation performed

This exact machinery (implemented as a throwaway prototype before being committed here) was validated two ways, entirely reusing physics already validated elsewhere in this package:

  1. Exact-to-floating-point amplitude check against AlignedSpinPNHamF2 (itself validated to ~1e-14 against real lalsimulation): built the same reconstruction on top of AlignedSpinPNHam's own dense RK4 trajectory (its dphi/dt=Omega RHS convention is identical), substituted the analytic Newtonian-order dOmega/dt = (96/5)*eta*Omega^(11/3) (matching AlignedSpinPNHamF2's own restricted-PN-order amplitude convention exactly, since a "restricted" PN waveform keeps the amplitude at leading order even when the phase is high-order), and got Ampl_FD agreeing with AlignedSpinPNHamF2.Ampl to full Float64 precision (ratio 1.0 to 15-16 significant digits) at 5 test frequencies. This pins down the (1/2) prefactor (a factor easy to drop by mistake – an earlier attempt without it was off by a smooth, frequency-dependent factor of ~1.3-2x, not a constant, which is what motivated deriving it from Maggiore's formula directly rather than guessing).
  2. Phase-shape check using the model's own true (non-Newtonian-restricted) dOmega/dt: matched AlignedSpinPNHamF2.Phi to ~2e-3 rad residual after removing a best-fit affine (a + b*f) term – expected, since the two models' t=0/phi=0 references differ by construction (this file's t* is measured from the f_min initial condition; AlignedSpinPNHamF2 anchors phase via its own t0/phiRef convention) and AlignedSpinPNHamF2 is independently known to omit AlignedSpinPNHam's quadrupole spin-spin content (see that file's own docstring), which would show up as exactly such a residual at this level.
  3. Self-consistency check on the real SEOBNRv5AlignedSpin PA table itself: interpolated Phi_TD(t*(f)) from the PA-fixed dense table agrees with an independent from-scratch RK4 re-integration (SEOBNRv5AlignedSpin.Phi called directly at the same t*) to ~0.02-0.04 rad out of a phase that has accumulated to ~1000+ rad by f=100Hz (config mc=8.0, eta=0.2479, chi1=0.3, chi2=-0.2, f_min=20) – a tiny, expected post-adiabatic-vs-full-dynamics residual (a known, already-documented approximation elsewhere in this package), not a defect in the interpolation itself.

GravitationalWaveforms.SEOBNRv5HM — Type
SEOBNRv5HM

SEOBNRv5HM in the frequency domain (aligned spins; modes (2,2), (2,1), (3,3), (3,2), (4,4), (4,3), the pyseobnr 0.3.7 default): the time-domain model of seobnrv5hm_td_modes turned into one table per mode by _seobv5hm_coeffs, the same way SEOBNRv5IMRAlignedSpinF2 does it for the (2,2) mode:

  • below a blend band f(t_a - 150 M) .. f(t_a - 90 M) (each mode's own frequency), the stationary-phase approximation on the mode's NQC-corrected phase and signed amplitude (cubic-Hermite root for the stationary time, as _eobimr_spa_eval);
  • above it, a direct Fourier transform of the mode from t_a - 1500 M (Planck-type turn-on) through 15 (2,2) damping times, tabulated on the FFT bins as U(f) = e^{2 pi i f t_a} h(f) and dU/df (real and imaginary parts, cubic Hermite: no trigonometric functions per frequency bin);
  • a C1 blend in between, and a C1 amplitude taper over the top 20% of each mode's band [.., 2.2 f_QNM].

PN order

The legacy generator's phasing/frequency coefficient arrays (pta0..pta7, fta0..fta7, plus the 6PN log terms ptl6/ftl6) stop at pta7/fta7 – i.e. 3.5PN order (LAL's own "twice-PN-order" convention: O=7 <=> 3.5PN) – confirmed directly from XLALSimInspiralTaylorT3Setup's switch (O) (case 7/-1 selects XLALSimInspiralPhasing3_7PN/XLALSimInspiralFrequency3_7PN, the highest available). This implementation hardcodes that same (highest-available) 3.5PN order – LAL exposes phase-order truncation as a run-time option, but this package's "closed-form PN phasing" design goal doesn't call for exposing that knob.

Parametrization: theta

Following the source exactly, define the dimensionless "reduced time" variable theta = (eta*td/(5*M))^(-1/8), where M is the total mass in seconds (= (mc/eta^(3/5)) * GMsun_over_c3) and td is (nominally) "time until coalescence". This package's t argument to Phi/Ampl matches LALSuite's choose_td_waveform/ SimInspiralChooseTDWaveform output convention exactly (t <= 0, coalescence nominally at t = 0), via td = t0_shift - t, where t0_shift corrects for a genuine subtlety below – it is not simply -t.

t0_shift: LAL's t = 0 is not the formula's own coalescence singularity

Naively, one might assume td = -t directly (t = 0 <=> the formal PN coalescence, where theta diverges) – this is wrong for TaylorT3, as cross-validation against real lalsimulation shows: comparing against real output showed a real, systematically growing discrepancy, present from the very first sample, that persisted even at pure-Newtonian (0PN) phasing (ruling out any higher-PN coefficient bug). The root cause: XLALSimInspiralTaylorT3PNEvolveOrbit's integration loop terminates the moment v >= v_isco = 1/sqrt(6) or the frequency series stops increasing (f <= fOld – this truncated 3.5PN series is well known to turn over well before v_isco for many mass ratios, confirmed directly for this ecosystem's own baseline masses), not when theta reaches its own formal singularity. LAL's output-array epoch-shift convention nonetheless always treats "one sample past the last generated sample" as t = 0, for bookkeeping – even though that point is really wherever the loop happened to be cut off, which is generically before the formula's own theta-diverges point.

t0_shift (computed once per waveform by _taylort3_t0_shift, a closed-form root-find – no ODE integration) is exactly that gap: theta_stop, the theta value at which LAL's own termination condition would trigger, mapped back to a time interval via t0_shift = (5*M/eta) * theta_stop^(-8). With this correction, t = 0 (LAL's own "coalescence") maps to theta = theta_stop (finite, physically meaningful – not theta = ∞), and t <= 0 never reaches the formula's actual singularity at all (that would require t = t0_shift > 0, outside this model's expected input domain) – a strictly better-behaved domain than the naive -t parametrization.

Phase convention: GW phase (2x orbital), not orbital phase

The source's phasing3 functions return orbital phase (the array is even named "ORBITAL_PHASE" in XLALSimInspiralTaylorT3PNEvolveOrbit), and the restricted-amplitude polarizations use cos(2*phi_orb)/sin(2*phi_orb) directly (see XLALSimInspiralPNPolarizationWaveforms's ampO=0 case: hp0 = -(1+ci^2)*cos(2*phi), hc0 = -2*ci*sin(2*phi)). To match this package's own IMRPhenomD/IMRPhenomXAS convention – where h ~ Ampl(f)*exp(i*Phi(f)), i.e. Phi IS the phase that appears directly inside the trig/exponential, not something that needs an extra factor of 2 at the call site – Phi here returns 2*phi_orb, the GW phase, not the bare orbital phase. This also makes Phi/Ampl mutually consistent for the "(2,2)-mode-only, face-on" restricted-amplitude convention documented on Ampl: h+ = -Ampl(t)*cos(Phi(t)), hx = -Ampl(t)*sin(Phi(t)) at zero inclination (matching IMRPhenomD.Ampl's own documented face-on convention).

The theta_lso reference scale (5PN+ log term)

The 5PN-and-higher phasing/frequency terms contain a term pta5*log(theta/theta_lso)*theta^5 (phasing) / an analogous 6PN log(2*theta) term (frequency) — theta_lso is a reference value of theta (not a physical cutoff enforced here), computed once per waveform from XLALSimInspiralTaylorLength: theta_lso = tn^(-1/8) where tn = -M*sign*integral(dE/dv / F(v), v0, vlso) - deltaT, v0 is the PN velocity parameter at f_min, and vlso = min(1/sqrt(6), (pi*M/(2*deltaT))^(1/3)) (almost always 1/sqrt(6), the Schwarzschild ISCO velocity, for realistic deltaT/masses — the Nyquist-velocity cap only binds for pathologically large deltaT). This requires numerically integrating the same 3PN energy/3.5PN flux functions LAL itself uses there (XLALSimInspiraldEt6/XLALSimInspiralFt7), which this file replicates with a from-scratch adaptive Simpson quadrature (_taylort3_* helpers below) rather than porting LAL's GSL-based adaptive Gauss-Kronrod call – the two should agree far beyond the precision this sub-leading, logarithmic-scale reference term needs (GSL's own default tolerance there is only 1e-7} relative). One genuine, documented LAL source quirk:XLALSimInspiralTaylorLength's localexpnCoeffsdEnergyFlux akEFstruct never assigns itsdETa5/dETa6fields (5PN/6PN spin-related energy terms that don't exist in the non-spinning 3PN energy expansionXLALSimInspiraldEt6is actually evaluating) before they're read insideXLALSimInspiraldEt6-- technically reading uninitialized stack memory in C. This implementation takes the physically-intended value,0.0, for both (consistent with them being 5PN/6PN spin-coupling coefficients that simply aren't part of any non-spinning PN energy expansion at this order) rather than attempting to reproduce whatever incidental garbage a particular compiler/stack state produced -- seetaylort3thetalso's docstring.fminanddeltaTare therefore real (if usually tiny-effect) keyword inputs toPhi/Amplhere, not just LAL-side data-generation settings; defaults (f_min=20.0,deltaT=1/4096`) match the other LALSuite comparisons.

GravitationalWaveforms.twist_up22 — Function
twist_up22(model::IMRPhenomXP, f, m1, m2, chi1x, chi1y, chi1z, chi2x, chi2y, chi2z,
           f_ref, inclination, phiRef, dL, phiRefOrbital = phiRef; kwargs...)

End-to-end IMRPhenomXP dominant-mode-precession waveform: builds the precession-frame setup (_xp_precession_setup), evaluates IMRPhenomXAS's aligned-spin (2,2) mode internally to get hAS = Ampl(...) * exp(im*Phi(...)), and twists it up via _xp_twist_up22 to return (hp, hc). f may be a scalar (returns a scalar (hp, hc) tuple, non-allocating beyond Ampl/ Phi's own scalar path) or an AbstractVector (returns same-length Vector{Complex{T}}s for hp/hc) – matching Ampl/Phi's own scalar-vs-array convention. See IMRPhenomXP's docstring for why this returns (hp, hc) directly rather than participating in the Ampl/Phi two-function contract.

m1, m2 are component masses in solar masses (m1 >= m2, this package's/file's mass-ordering convention – see _xp_precession_setup's docstring); chi1*/chi2* are dimensionless spin components in the L-aligned (source) frame at f_ref; inclination is the angle between the orbital angular momentum L and the line of sight at f_ref; dL is the luminosity distance in Gpc (same convention as Ampl(::IMRPhenomXAS, ...)).

phiRef vs phiRefOrbital

phiRef feeds _xp_precession_setup's line-of-sight-vector construction (Nx/Ny, hence thetaJN) – see that function's docstring for why it needs phiRef explicitly. phiRefOrbital is passed through to Phi(::IMRPhenomXAS, ...)'s own phiRef keyword, which is a simple external constant-phase offset on the aligned-spin (2,2) phase (see that function's docstring: "no internal time-of-coalescence alignment is performed"). In LALSuite's own construction these coincide (a single phiRef value feeds both roles, see LALSimIMRPhenomX_precession.c lines 865-866 vs the phase alignment in LALSimIMRPhenomX.c), so phiRefOrbital defaults to phiRef here for physical consistency by default; it is exposed separately because this package's Phi(::IMRPhenomXAS, ...) already has its own independent, decoupled phiRef/t0 external-alignment convention (unlike LALSuite's tightly-coupled internal phase construction), and overriding it independently is occasionally useful (e.g. re-aligning hAS's phase without moving the precession-frame geometry).

Keyword arguments

  • fcutPar = 0.3: forwarded to both Ampl(::IMRPhenomXAS, ...) and Phi(::IMRPhenomXAS, ...).
  • GMsun_over_c3, GMsun_over_c2_Gpc: same convention as Ampl/Phi(::IMRPhenomXAS, ...).

No parameter-domain assertions run here – this is the hot path; see validate_phenomxp_precession_params/validate_phenomxas_params for opt-in host-side checks.
